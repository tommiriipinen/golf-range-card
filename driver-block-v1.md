# Driver block — launch first, speed second

**Replaces:** all prior driver content in the range card.
**Duration:** 12 weeks. Launch work is weeks 1–8, speed work runs 1–12 in parallel on separate days.
**Baseline (63-shot slice, 6 May + 4 Jul 2026, n=15 drivers):**

| Metric | Baseline |
|---|---|
| Club speed | 85.4 mph |
| Ball speed | 127.7 mph |
| Smash | 1.495 |
| Attack angle | +0.8° |
| Dynamic loft | 9.9° |
| Spin loft | 9.1° |
| Launch | 8.6° |
| Spin | 1862 rpm |
| Peak height | 8.3 m |
| Landing angle | 16.7° |
| Carry | 143.0 m |
| Total | 177.2 m |

**The problem in one line:** spin loft of 9.1° with a 10.5° head means you are *delofting* the driver at impact. The 5 wood delivers 13.2° of spin loft and out-carries the driver by 6.5 m on 5.7 mph less ball speed.

**Why launch is the priority:** within these 15 drives, carry correlates with launch at 0.77 and spin at 0.65, but with club speed at only 0.51. The longest drive of the set (182.6 m) was the highest-spin, highest-launch one — not the fastest.

## Block A — Launch conditions (primary)

### A0. Equipment changes — do these before drill one

These are free yardage and they change the numbers you'll be training against, so make them first.

1. **Loft up the hosel.** Elyte OptiFit — go from 10.5° to 11.5° (or 12° if available). Worth ~1° of dynamic loft and ~150–200 rpm immediately.
2. **Tee height.** Tee so the equator of the ball sits level with the crown, i.e. roughly half the ball above the top line. Most likely you are teeing at iron-ish height, which makes an ascending strike physically awkward.
3. **Ball position.** Off the lead heel — genuinely forward, not "forward for you". Use an alignment stick on the ground perpendicular to the target line at your lead heel for the first three sessions.
4. **Ball model — test, do not assume.** TP5 is on the low-spin end. Given you need spin, put a sleeve of Pro V1 or Chrome Soft in the bag for one Trackman session and compare driver spin directly. Keep whichever wins. Do not change ball model and swing pattern in the same session.

Re-baseline on Trackman *after* these four, before starting A1. The equipment alone may move launch 1.5–2° and spin 300 rpm, and you want to train against the real remaining gap.

### A1. Weeks 1–3 — build the ascending strike

Goal: attack angle from +0.8° to +2.5° without losing centre-face contact.

- **Tilt-and-hold setup rehearsal** — 10 reps, no ball. Set up, add 5–8° of trail-side spine tilt so the lead shoulder sits visibly higher, feel about 55% of weight on the trail foot at address. Hold 5 seconds. This is the position, not a swing thought.
- **Forward-tee gate** — 15 balls. Ball on a normal tee; a second tee pushed nearly flush into the turf about 20 cm ahead on the target line. The clubhead must miss the forward tee. Clipping it means you are still descending. Start at 60% effort.
- **Step-through finish** — 10 balls. Full swing, then let the trail foot walk through past the lead foot after impact. Encourages the pivot to move left while the club exits up, which is the opposite of your early-extension pattern.
- **Half-speed loft rehearsal** — 10 balls at 50% effort, trying to see the ball launch *visibly higher* than normal. You are calibrating a feel, not hitting shots. Ignore direction entirely.

**Session shape:** 45 balls total, ~35 minutes. Twice a week. Never on a speed day.

### A2. Weeks 4–6 — add dynamic loft

Goal: dynamic loft from ~10° to 14°, spin loft to ~11.5°.

Attack angle alone will not fix this — raising AoA *reduces* spin loft. Dynamic loft has to climb faster than attack angle does.

- **Handle-back impact feel** — 12 balls. The feel is that the grip end has *not* run ahead of the clubhead at impact. Exaggerate it: try to feel the clubhead passing the hands before the ball. It will feel like a scoop and it will not be one. Check on Trackman, not by feel.
- **High-window shots** — 15 balls. Pick a tree or a point on the range netting and try to launch the ball *over* it. Height is the only success criterion.
- **Trail-hand-only slow swings** — 10 reps, no ball, half speed. Keeps the trail wrist from flattening early into shaft lean.
- **Full swings with numbers up** — 15 balls, standing where you can see launch angle after each. Target 12°+. Say the number out loud before you look. Building the feel-to-real map is the point.

**Session shape:** 40 balls + rehearsals, ~40 minutes. Twice a week.

### A3. Weeks 7–8 — consolidate under pressure

- **Nine-shot gate test** — 9 balls, one at a time, full pre-shot routine, 60 seconds between. Pass = 6 of 9 at launch ≥ 12° and spin ≥ 2200 rpm.
- **Alternating clubs** — driver, 5 wood, driver, 5 wood, 12 balls. The 5 wood already has the right delivery; you are borrowing its feel. Notice what changes between the two.
- **Course-sim tee shots** — 10 balls, each to a different imagined fairway width, alternating targets. No swing thoughts allowed.

## Block B — Speed training (secondary, weeks 1–12)

Run this on separate days from Block A. Three sessions a week, non-consecutive, roughly 25 minutes. Speed days are speed days — no ball flight analysis, no swing thoughts, no scoring.

**Equipment:** a graduated speed-stick set (SuperSpeed or equivalent — light / medium / heavy). If you'd rather not buy: an alignment stick works as the light implement and an upside-down driver gripped at the hosel works as the medium, but the progression is much cruder and the results will be too.

### Warm-up — non-negotiable, 6 minutes

Arm circles, trunk rotations, hip openers, then 10 slow half-swings with the medium stick building to 60%. Overspeed work on cold tissue is how people injure ribs and lower backs. If anything hurts, stop the session — not just the set.

### Weeks 1–3 — baseline protocol

| Set | Implement | Reps |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Light | 6 dominant, 6 non-dominant |
| 2 | Medium | 6 dominant, 6 non-dominant |
| 3 | Heavy | 6 dominant, 6 non-dominant |
| 4 | Driver | 3 swings, full effort, no ball |

Non-dominant reps matter — they are most of where the neuromuscular gain comes from. Do not skip them because they feel silly.

### Weeks 4–6 — add instability

Same structure, but sets 1 and 2 become **step-change swings**: start with feet together, step into the lead foot during transition, swing through. Set 3 stays standard.

### Weeks 7–9 — add kneeling work

Sets 1 and 2 performed from both knees on a mat. Removes the ground push and forces the upper body to generate speed. Set 3 standard, set 4 goes to 5 driver swings.

### Weeks 10–12 — max effort

Full protocol, every rep at genuine maximum. Add a fifth set: 5 driver swings with a ball, at full speed, accepting whatever happens to the ball. Record club speed on all five.

### Measurement

Record max club speed on the **light stick, dominant hand, set 1 rep 6** every session. That is your speed metric. Do not use driver speed on the range as the tracking number — it's contaminated by whatever you're working on in Block A.

## Targets and checkpoints

Benchmark on Trackman every two weeks: 10 drivers, full routine, log all of the below.

| Dynamic loft | 9.9° | 12.0° | 14.0° | 15.0° |
| Spin loft | 9.1° | 10.5° | 11.5° | 12.5° |

| Spin | 1862 | 2100 | 2300 | 2450 |

| Landing angle | 16.7° | 26° | 33° | 37° |
| Club speed | 85.4 | 86.5 | 88.0 | 90.0 |
| Carry | 143 m | 165 m | 182 m | 195 m |

**Gate:** do not advance from A1 to A2 until attack angle averages ≥ +2.0° across a 10-shot benchmark. Do not advance from A2 to A3 until spin ≥ 2200 rpm on 6 of 10.

**Expected split of the gain:** roughly 45–50 m from launch conditions, 12–18 m from speed. If after week 8 the carry number has barely moved but launch and spin are on target, the problem is elsewhere and worth a fitting rather than more reps.

## Notes and caveats

- The 5 wood comparison rests on only 5 shots across two sessions. Treat the 4.0 mph speed gap as directional.
- If any of the baseline shots are virtual-range strokes, spin may be estimated rather than measured. Check the `reducedAccuracy` flag on this slice before treating 1862 rpm as gospel — if spin is estimated, re-baseline on a real range or with a radar-confirmed session before committing to the loft change in A0.
- Your known pattern is early extension → low point behind the ball → thin high-face strikes. Block A works *with* that chain rather than against it: an ascending strike wants the low point behind the ball. What it does not want is the shaft lean. Watch that A1 does not quietly reintroduce early extension as a way of getting the ball higher.
- Face-to-path stays closed in this data. Do not chase it during this block. Launch is the bigger lever and the two fixes interfere with each other.
