★Driver — launch first, speed second▾

Twelve weeks. Launch work is weeks 1–8, speed work runs 1–12 in parallel on separate days. This replaces every earlier driver drill on this card.

The problem in one line

Spin loft of 9.1° with a 10.5° head means you are delofting the driver at impact. The 5 wood delivers 13.2° of spin loft and out-carries the driver by 6.5 m on 5.7 mph less ball speed.

Why launch is the priority

Within these 15 drives, carry correlates with launch at 0.77 and spin at 0.65, but with club speed at only 0.51. The longest drive of the set — 182.6 m — was the highest-spin, highest-launch one. Not the fastest.

A0 · Equipment, before drill one

These are free yardage and they change the numbers you will be training against, so make them first.

  1. Loft up the hosel. Elyte OptiFit — 10.5° to 11.5°, or 12° if it is available. Worth about 1° of dynamic loft and 150–200 rpm immediately.
  2. Tee height. Tee so the equator of the ball sits level with the crown — roughly half the ball above the top line. Iron-ish tee height makes an ascending strike physically awkward.
  3. Ball position. Off the lead heel, genuinely forward — not “forward for you”. Alignment stick on the ground, perpendicular to the target line at your lead heel, for the first three sessions.
  4. Ball model — test, do not assume. TP5 sits on the low-spin end and you need spin. Put a sleeve of Pro V1 or Chrome Soft in the bag for one Trackman session and compare driver spin directly. Keep whichever wins. Never change ball model and swing pattern in the same session.
Re-baseline before A1

Take a fresh Trackman baseline after those four. Equipment alone may move launch 1.5–2° and spin 300 rpm, and you want to train against the real remaining gap.

★ALaunch conditions · weeks 1–8▾

The primary block. Twice a week per phase, never on a speed day. Gates below decide when you move on — not the calendar.

A1 · Weeks 1–3 — build the ascending strike

Goal

Attack angle from +0.8° to +2.5°, without losing centre-face contact.

Session shape: 45 balls, about 35 minutes, twice a week. Never on a speed day.

A2 · Weeks 4–6 — add dynamic loft

Goal

Dynamic loft from ~10° to 14°, spin loft to ~11.5°. Attack angle alone will not do it — raising AoA reduces spin loft, so dynamic loft has to climb faster than attack angle does.

Session shape: 40 balls plus rehearsals, about 40 minutes, twice a week.

A3 · Weeks 7–8 — consolidate under pressure

Gates

Do not advance A1 → A2 until attack angle averages ≥ +2.0° across a 10-shot benchmark. Do not advance A2 → A3 until spin is ≥ 2200 rpm on 6 of 10.

★BSpeed training · weeks 1–12▾

Separate days from Block A. Three sessions a week, non-consecutive, about 25 minutes. Speed days are speed days — no ball flight analysis, no swing thoughts, no scoring.

Equipment

A graduated speed-stick set — SuperSpeed or equivalent, light / medium / heavy. If you would rather not buy: an alignment stick works as the light implement and an upside-down driver gripped at the hosel as the medium, but the progression is much cruder and the results will be too.

Warm-up — 6 minutes, non-negotiable

Arm circles, trunk rotations, hip openers, then 10 slow half-swings with the medium stick building to 60%. Overspeed work on cold tissue is how people injure ribs and lower backs. If anything hurts, stop the session — not just the set.

Non-dominant reps are most of where the neuromuscular gain comes from. Do not skip them because they feel silly.

Progression

Weeks 4–6 · add instabilitySame structure, but sets 1 and 2 become step-change swings: feet together, step into the lead foot during transition, swing through. Set 3 stays standard.
Weeks 7–9 · add kneeling workSets 1 and 2 from both knees on a mat. Removes the ground push and forces the upper body to generate speed. Set 3 standard, set 4 goes to 5 driver swings.
Weeks 10–12 · max effortFull protocol, every rep at genuine maximum. Add a fifth set: 5 driver swings with a ball at full speed, accepting whatever happens to the ball. Record club speed on all five.
Measurement

Record max club speed on the light stick, dominant hand, set 1 rep 6 every session. That is your speed metric. Do not use driver speed on the range as the tracking number — it is contaminated by whatever you are working on in Block A.

★NDriver caveats▾

The 5 wood comparison is thinFive shots across two sessions. Treat the 4.0 mph speed gap as directional, not as a measurement.
Spin may be estimated, not measuredIf any baseline shots are virtual-range strokes, check the reducedAccuracy flag on the slice before treating 1862 rpm as gospel. If spin is estimated, re-baseline on a real range or a radar-confirmed session before committing to the A0 loft change.
Block A works with your existing pattern, not against itEarly extension → low point behind the ball → thin high-face strikes. An ascending strike actually wants the low point behind the ball; what it does not want is the shaft lean. Watch that A1 does not quietly reintroduce early extension as a way of getting the ball higher.
Leave face-to-path aloneIt stays closed in this data and it is the smaller lever. Launch is the bigger one, and the two fixes interfere with each other.

01Transfer protocol▾

Your practice swing already works. The ball takes it away. These six rules govern every block.

  1. Rehearsal and shot are one unit. Rehearse, step in, hit within five seconds.
  2. Same speed both times. A slower rehearsal is not a rehearsal.
  3. Look 10 cm ahead of the ball, or at its front edge. Never the back.
  4. Grip pressure 4 out of 10 — check it is still 4 at the top.
  5. Cut your time over the ball. Deliberation is tension, and tension is how the ball wins.
  6. Score the swing, not the shot. Rate 1–5 on how close it felt to the rehearsal. Ignore where it went.

APosture & ball transfer▾

First block every session, never cut. Face spray every fifth shot — strike location is the scoreboard, not ball flight.

Pick three, not nine

One of them is always the chair drill. If the rehearsal divot test came back clean, weight toward the transfer four.

BChipping low point▾

Narrow stance, feet slightly open, ball just back of centre, handle 2–4 cm ahead, 70% pressure lead foot — and it stays there.

Graduation

At 16 of 20 on the line drill, add the landing ladder — towels at 3, 6 and 9 m, five balls each, random order.

CLow point confirmation▾

The goal, restated

Not a divot. A mark after the ball, however shallow. A bruise or a scuff counts. If the club leaves nothing, low point is still at or behind the ball. If it scuffs — you are done.

Grass only

On a mat the club skids, so contact 2 cm behind the ball still flies. Mats cannot teach low point. Every drill in this block happens on grass.

Desensitisation ladder. Tap your current rung. Move up only when the current one is boring — two or three sessions each.

Throughout: grip pressure 4/10 — tight grips send shock into the wrists, soft ones absorb it. Discomfort is expected; pain is not. Stop and get it looked at.

Drills, once the ladder is done

Before blaming your swing for spin

Check the stamped loft. A 23–24° hollow-body "5-iron" at 75 mph will not stop quickly regardless of delivery. Cheaper levers: clean grooves, dry face and ball, centred strikes.

04On the course▾

For now

Take the divot question off the table entirely. Play the strike you have — your iron results are already good on average, and a shallow delivery is a legitimate way to play golf, not a fault to fix mid-round. The ladder belongs on the range. Manufacturing a divot on the course is exactly how you produce the duffs that started the flinch.
